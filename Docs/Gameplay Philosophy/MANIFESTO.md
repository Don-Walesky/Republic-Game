# The Republic Manifesto

**Republic is not a game about ruling people.**

**It is a persistent multiplayer simulation about carrying the responsibility of statecraft, building a nation, and sustaining a sovereign country in a living world.**

---

## The North Star

Every player governs their own procedurally generated country with one sovereign objective:

> **BUILD, STRENGTHEN, PROTECT AND SUSTAIN THEIR COUNTRY.**

The **country itself** is the central object of the simulation. Its land, its people, its institutions, its economy, and its security are what live and endure. The **Presidential Office** is the player's command interface and command centre—the vantage point from which the burden of leadership is carried.

---

## Core Tenets

### 1. State Capacity Over Handouts
> *"National Yield is not free stuff given to the player. It is the measurable output and accumulating capacity of a functioning state."*

The state does not receive magic gifts. Wealth, security, food, and innovation are the earned fruits of state capacity, wise infrastructure, and sound governance.

### 2. The Living, Unbroken World
> *"Revenue is continuous. Development is continuous. Politics is continuous. The player is not."*

Republic exists on a shared global simulation clock aligned to real-world time (West Africa Time, WAT / UTC+1). The world does not pause when the president sleeps or steps away. History moves relentlessly forward.

### 3. Every Choice Demands a Price
> *"There are no silver bullets in statecraft. Every important decision has pros and cons."*

Competence clashes with loyalty; military strength strains civilian budgets; rapid growth challenges social stability. Leadership is the art of navigating necessary trade-offs.

---

Whenever we debate a mechanic, whenever we question a system, whenever we evaluate a feature:

**Ask: Does this reinforce the survival and strength of the country? Does it confront the player with the authentic trade-offs of governance? Does it honor our persistent, shared world?**

If the answer is yes, it belongs in Republic.
